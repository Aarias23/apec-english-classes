Habit, or happening right now?

Grammar · Practice
Common errors — enrichment beyond the book

Watch out for these three

The most frequent slips students make with these two tenses.

Typical mistakes

  • Forgetting -s in 3rd person: "She go" → She goes.
  • Dropping be: "They watching TV" → They are watching TV.
  • Using -ing with state verbs: "I'm knowing" → I know. Verbs like know, like, want, believe almost never take -ing.

Signal words to underline

alwaysusuallyevery day nowright nowat the moment Look!Listen!

Pair practice: one student says an action with "right now"; the partner reframes it as a habit using "usually".

Reading
Skill focus: using the introduction — p.18

Predict before you read

The book's key strategy: the introduction of a travel blog signals the topic, the place, and the tone of the whole post.

How to apply it

  • Read only the first line or two before reading the full text.
  • Ask: who is writing? Where are they? What will the reader learn?
  • Use those clues to predict vocabulary and content before reading in detail.

From the book: Sam's blog (p.18)

Sam writes a blog post about a trip to China around February 9th–10th, describing the Spring Festival at Shanghai Botanical Garden, a visit to Shanghai Disney Resort, traditional Chinese clothing, Lion Dances, and a New Year meal of rice cakes, fish, and spring rolls. Reading · Enrichment
Same strategy, a new example

Try the strategy on a new blog

An original introduction (not from the book) so students practice the same skill on unfamiliar text.

"Hi everyone! I'm writing this from a small café in Kyoto, and right now the cherry blossoms are falling outside the window like pink snow. This week I'm exploring temples, trying street food, and learning three new Japanese words a day…"

Ask the class

Where is the writer? Which tense describes what's happening right now? What do you expect to read next?

Global Skills
Communication & collaboration — p.20

Plans that work for everyone

A preview of the unit's Global Skills focus, connected to today's grammar.

Discussion prompt

In groups, students describe their usual weekend routine (simple present) and negotiate one activity they could all do together this weekend (present progressive for a shared plan).

Why it matters

Making a plan that works for everyone means listening to routines that differ (simple present) and agreeing on one shared action (present progressive) — the exact grammar contrast from today's lesson, used for real collaboration.
